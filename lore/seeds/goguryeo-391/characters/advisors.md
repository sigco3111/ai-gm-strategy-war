# Characters — Advisors

> A handful of named figures in the Goguryeo court in 391. The seed carries a minimal roster; the GM and player may add, retire, or invent characters as the game progresses.

## [CHUNK: character -- Sosurim]
# triggers: sosurim, 소수림왕, reformer, regent, buddhism, 태학
# priority: 8
**Sosurim (소수림왕, 371–384)** — technically the 17th monarch of Goguryeo, but a regent for the young Gogugyang rather than a direct predecessor to Gwanggaeto. He **died in 384**, seven years before game start. But his legacy is the foundation of the kingdom Gwanggaeto inherits. Sosurim introduced Buddhism (372), established the Taehak national school, and reformed the military after the 371 defeat. Every competent policy of Gwanggaeto's reign is, in part, Sosurim's policy.

The GM may invoke Sosurim as a **ghost advisor** — quoting him, invoking his reforms, or having the player's ministers invoke him in council. Sosurim's *시호* (posthumous name) means "to refine and prosper" — a fitting description of his regency.

## [CHUNK: character -- Gogugyang]
# triggers: gogukyang, 고국양왕, 384, 391, gogugwon's son, military king
# priority: 7
**Gogugyang (고국양왕, 故國壤王, r. 384–391)** — Gwanggaeto's father. Died in 391, leaving the throne to his son. Gogugyang's reign was **militarily active and politically fraught**:

- **385** — invaded Later Yan. Result unclear; possibly a tactical success, possibly a draw.
- **386** — invaded Baekje. Result unclear; sources are silent on outcome.
- **384–391** — consolidated the northern court at Gungnae Seong after the 371 loss of Pyongyang.
- **386** — named Gwanggaeto crown prince, securing the succession.

Gogugyang is a **dead king whose shadow is still long**. The generals who served him are the same generals who will serve Gwanggaeto. Their loyalty is to the throne, not necessarily to the new boy king. Gwanggaeto must earn their trust, displace them, or work around them.

## [CHUNK: character -- Gubu]
# triggers: gubu, 구부왕, crown prince, predeceased, 386
# priority: 5
**Gubu (구부왕)** — briefly crown prince before Gwanggaeto. Succeeded Gogugyang in 384 as a young king (per some accounts), predeceased in 386 when the title passed to Gwanggaeto. The exact circumstances are murky in historical record. The seed carries Gubu as a **cautionary reference**: succession in Goguryeo has not always been orderly, and a young crown prince's death is not unprecedented. Gwanggaeto is seventeen and healthy, but the precedent reminds the court that princes die young.

(The GM may treat Gubu as historical fact, vague rumor, or eliminate him entirely from the campaign — the seed's tone is realistic, not strictly historical.)
