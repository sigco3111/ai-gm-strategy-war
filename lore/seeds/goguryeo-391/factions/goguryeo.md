# Factions — Goguryeo 391

## [CHUNK: faction -- Goguryeo]
# triggers: goguryeo, 고구려, goryeo, koguryo, 고왕
# priority: 9
**Goguryeo (고구려)** — the player's kingdom, ruled from Gungnae Seong on the Yalu. The 19th monarch, **Gwanggaeto the Great** (광개토대왕), ascended in May 391 at age 17. The kingdom is recovering from the disastrous 371 defeat by Baekje that killed his grandfather Gogugwon and sacked the previous capital. His father Gogugyang spent the 384–391 reign at war with Later Yan and Baekje.

Goguryeo's strength is its **military tradition**: a reformed standing army (introduced under King Sosurim, 371–384) supplemented by tribal militia drawn from the rugged Manchurian hinterland. Its weakness is its **economy** — the kingdom's southern territories, the most agriculturally rich, were lost to Baekje in 371 and have not been recovered. The treasury is thin. The court is factional. The legitimacy of a 17-year-old king is not yet proven.

**Player character**: Gwanggaeto. Heir: Georyeon (거련).

## [CHUNK: faction -- Goguryeo Reform Legacy]
# triggers: sosurim, reform, buddhism, 태학, military reform
# priority: 7
The **Sosurim reforms** (371–384) form the foundation on which the player builds. King Sosurim — Gwanggaeto's great-uncle, who ruled as regent before Gogugyang — instituted:

- **Buddhism as state religion** (372 CE). The first Buddhist temples appeared; monks served as scholars, advisors, and a counterweight to the shamanic aristocracy.
- **National schools (태학, Taehak)** for the training of young nobles. This created a class of literate, loyal administrators who would staff the later imperial bureaucracy.
- **Military reform** — replacing tribal levies with a more disciplined standing army, ranked by merit, supplied by a state granary. The 371 defeat had exposed the limits of tribal warfare; Sosurim's reforms were the answer.

These reforms are not the player's achievement, but they are his inheritance. Every action Gwanggaeto takes sits on top of them.
