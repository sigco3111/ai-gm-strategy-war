# Factions — Later Yan

## [CHUNK: faction -- Later Yan]
# triggers: later yan, 후연, 후연, murong, xianbei, 모용
# priority: 8
**Later Yan (후연, 後燕)** — the Xianbei state to the west, ruling much of present-day Liaoning under the **Murong (모용) clan**. In 391, the ruler is **Murong Chui (모용최, 慕容垂)**, one of the most capable generals of his age. Later Yan is currently the strongest single power in the region — it has absorbed the Former Yan state and controls the Liao River basin and the western approaches to Goguryeo.

Later Yan's relationship with Goguryeo is **ambiguous and dangerous**. In 385, Gogugyang invaded Later Yan — a bold but unsuccessful move that may have left a residue of grievance. The Murong are expansionist but pragmatic. They may accept tribute, they may demand vassalage, they may invade. The player must read Murong Chui's intentions carefully and either buy time, forge an alliance, or strike first.

**Ruler**: Murong Chui. Strategic priority: absorb Liaodong and the Korean peninsula fringes.

## [CHUNK: faction -- Xianbei Steppe Politics]
# triggers: xianbei, steppe, 모용, 봉희, nomad, cavalry
# priority: 6
The **Xianbei** are a confederation of steppe peoples north of China proper. Several Xianbei states have risen and fallen in the past century — Former Yan, Later Yan, Northern Yan, Western Yan, Southern Yan. The pattern: a charismatic Murong leader unifies the confederation, expands, then fragments on succession. The player who watches Later Yan's internal politics may see opportunities for alliance with disaffected Murong princes, or for war when a succession crisis erupts.

The Xianbei are **cavalry people**. Their military advantage over Korean kingdoms is mobility — a Xianbei army can strike deep and withdraw before a Korean force can respond. Counter-strategies: fortress networks (see regions), cavalry corps of one's own, alliance with other steppe powers.
