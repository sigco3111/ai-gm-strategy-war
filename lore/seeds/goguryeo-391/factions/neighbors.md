# Factions — Neighboring Powers

Four secondary nations that surround Goguryeo. Each is a potential ally, a threat, or a target.

## [CHUNK: faction -- Silla]
# triggers: silla, 신라, kims, gyeongju
# priority: 7
**Silla (신라)** — the southeastern kingdom, ruled from Gyeongju. In 391, Silla is smaller and less militarized than Goguryeo or Baekje, but **rich and stable** — a confederation of six villages (육부) that has converted to Buddhism and developed a sophisticated aristocracy. Silla's strategic position is defensive: sandwiched between Goguryeo and Baekje, with a long eastern coast.

Silla is the **natural anti-Baekje ally** for Goguryeo. A Goguryeo-Silla alliance would pincer Baekje between two fronts. Silla's price: protection from Baekje, possibly a Goguryeo prince married into the Silla royal house, possibly territorial concessions. The player should weigh the cost of alliance against the freedom of unilateral action.

## [CHUNK: faction -- Gaya]
# triggers: gaya, 가야, geocha, confederacy, iron, maritime
# priority: 6
**Gaya (가야)** — a confederacy of small maritime states along the southern coast, centered on Geumgwan Gaya (금관가야, modern Gimhae). Gaya's strength is **iron** and **maritime trade** — it sits on the best iron ore deposits on the peninsula and on the sea lanes between Baekje, Wa, and Silla. Its weakness is political fragmentation — Gaya has never unified under a single king, and its component polities often play one another off.

Gaya is a **port-and-iron supplier**, not a military power. The player might court Gaya for iron (to equip a larger army), for shipyards (to build a navy), or for a staging ground against Baekje. Gaya's price: iron, port access, or protection from Wa raids.

## [CHUNK: faction -- Wa]
# triggers: wa, 왜, japan, 야마토, archipelago
# priority: 5
**Wa (왜)** — the peoples of the Japanese archipelago, occasionally unified under a Yamato court but more often a loose collection of polities sending raiders and traders across the sea. In 391, the Wa presence on the peninsula is **thin and opportunistic** — they ally with Baekje against Silla and Goguryeo, raid the southern coast, and trade iron and horses for Korean silk and gold.

The Wa are **not** a direct threat to Goguryeo's core in 391. They are a maritime nuisance and a potential trade partner. The player might build a navy to extend Goguryeo's reach southward, or ignore the Wa entirely.

## [CHUNK: faction -- Eastern Buyeo]
# triggers: eastern buyeo, 동부여, buyeo, 부여, dongbuyeo
# priority: 6
**Eastern Buyeo (동부여)** — a people northeast of Goguryeo, formerly a major power that has been declining for a century. By 391, Eastern Buyeo is **weak and fragmented**, a tempting target. Its territories overlap with the eastern Yalu basin and the forests of what is now Russian Primorsky Krai.

Eastern Buyeo is Goguryeo's ancestral homeland — the original Go house migrated from Buyeo. A campaign against Eastern Buyeo would be partly a **reunification** of the Goguryeo people. It would also provoke the **Sushen** tribes further north and risk a war on a second front. The player should weigh expansion against consolidation.
